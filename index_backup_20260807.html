Core Contribution

PhD Thesis: Cross-Layer GPU Information Leakage

My thesis examines information leakage across three abstraction layers—microarchitectural resources, interconnect fabrics, LLM inference—demonstrating that no single mitigation eliminates all channels. The unifying finding is that GPU systems consistently expose measurable side-channel signals regardless of the abstraction level at which the attacker observes.

Microarchitectural Layer — 44.7 Mbps covert channel

Extended binary contention to four-level signaling on NVIDIA DGX A100. Achieved 44 Mbps (1 GPU) and 340 Mbps (8 GPUs). Behavioral randomization via SFU function combinations reduces ML detectability from 100% to 36%.

Interconnect Layer — NVLink attack surface

First study to expose NVLink contention across V100, A100, and H200. Covert channel: 6.33–9.90 Kbps with negligible error rates. Application fingerprinting via NVLink latency traces: 96.2% accuracy across 25 apps. Temporal and spatial partitioning countermeasures evaluated.

Application Layer — LLM inference leakage

Unprivileged NVML telemetry alone — no kernel-, CUDA-API-, or profiler-level tracing — reveals model identity and prompt semantics. Model fingerprinting: 100% at single-tenant (degrading to 83.8% under four-way contention). Prompt-family leakage: weaker and architecture-dependent, significant in 18 of 35 tested cells. Output duration is a separable second leakage channel; telemetry-layer mitigations evaluated.

SideLink: Exposing NVLink to Covert and Side-Channel Attacks

Issa Baddour, Somitra Kumar Sanadhya, Dip Sankar Banerjee

Journal of Hardware and Systems Security (Special Issue)

DOI →

NVLink is a high-bandwidth interconnect in NVIDIA GPU systems that has become essential for AI workloads in data centers. Despite its high bandwidth, we show that NVLink exhibits measurable contention characteristics that enable both covert and side-channel attacks. We evaluate SideLink across NVIDIA's Hopper (H200), Ampere (A100), and Volta (V100) architectures, achieving covert channel bandwidths of 8.29 Kbps, 9.90 Kbps, and 6.33 Kbps respectively with negligible error rates. We further implement an application fingerprinting side-channel, achieving a maximum accuracy of 96.2% across 25 dual-GPU applications. We propose and evaluate temporal and spatial partitioning countermeasures for NVLink, analyzing their effectiveness and performance overhead.

An Improved Micro-Architectural Covert-Channel Attack on GPUs

Issa Baddour, Somitra Kumar Sanadhya, Dip Sankar Banerjee

Journal of Hardware and Systems Security

DOI →

We address two major challenges in GPU covert-channel research: improving bandwidth and evading detection. We present an enhanced mechanism that extends binary contention-based communication to a four-level signaling scheme, achieving error-free communication at over 44 Mbps on a single GPU and over 340 Mbps across 8 GPUs on an NVIDIA DGX A100 system. We also systematically construct covert channels using every available function on the special function units (SFUs) and introduce behavioral randomization by dynamically combining these configurations. This randomized strategy achieves up to 38 Mbps (single GPU) and 274 Mbps (8 GPUs) with BER under 10%, and significantly reduces ML-based detectability compared to traditional approaches.

Side-Channel Leakage in Large Language Model Inference on GPUs

Issa Baddour, Somitra Kumar Sanadhya, Dip Sankar Banerjee

Under Review — AAAI 2027

We study whether unprivileged NVML telemetry alone — no kernel-, CUDA-API-, or profiler-level tracing — can be used to infer hidden properties of LLM inference. Collecting telemetry traces from seven LLMs (OPT-1.3B, OPT-IML-1.3B, Mistral-7B-Instruct, Phi-2, Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct, TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat, Falcon-rw-1B) across five occupancy conditions and three physically distinct hosts (Hopper H200 and Ampere A100, including a commercial-cloud A100) under realistic conditions—allowing variable input lengths, stochastic decoding, and natural termination—we evaluate two tasks: (i) model fingerprinting, identifying the executing model; and (ii) prompt-family inference, recovering high-level semantic categories of inputs. Model fingerprinting reaches 100% accuracy at single-tenant, robust to classifier choice and transferring to held-out prompts and hardware. Under real multi-tenant contention accuracy degrades (to 83.8% at four co-tenants), and a held-out-co-tenant analysis shows contended numbers bound rather than isolate victim-only leakage — single-tenant is the clean result. Prompt-family leakage is weaker and architecture-dependent, statistically established (bootstrap CI) in about half the tested cells (18 of 35), not all models. We further identify output duration as a separable second leakage channel and evaluate two telemetry-layer mitigations: calibrated noise injection and polling-rate reduction.

Execution-Level Covert Channels on GPUs

expand_more

44 Mbps (1 GPU) · 340 Mbps (8 GPUs) · 36% ML detectability after evasion

Problem: Can co-resident GPU processes communicate covertly at practical bandwidths while evading ML-based detection? Approach: We reverse-engineered warp and block scheduling on the NVIDIA A100 and extended binary contention to a four-level signaling scheme, systematically exploiting Special Function Unit (SFU) behavior with dynamic randomization to obscure consistent usage patterns. Threat model: Multi-tenant cloud GPUs, e.g. shared AI inference nodes where attacker and victim co-reside on the same physical GPU.

Side-Channel Leakage in LLM Inference on GPUs

expand_more

100% single-tenant fingerprinting · 7 LLMs · realistic stochastic decoding · Under Review, AAAI 2027

Problem: Can an unprivileged co-tenant infer which LLM is running, or the semantic category of its prompts, from GPU telemetry alone? Approach: We profiled seven LLMs using only unprivileged NVML telemetry — no kernel-, CUDA-API-, or profiler-level tracing — across multiple hosts and occupancy levels under realistic, stochastic decoding. Key finding: Model fingerprinting reaches 100% accuracy at single-tenant, degrading to 83.8% under four-way contention; prompt-family leakage is weaker and architecture-dependent, significant in 18 of 35 tested cells. Defenses: Two telemetry-layer mitigations evaluated — calibrated noise injection and polling-rate reduction.

FPGA-Accelerated SHA-256 Password Recovery via Partial Reconfiguration

expand_more

Graduate Course Project — IIT Jodhpur, 2021 · target <50 ms PR overhead (simulated)

Problem: Brute-force recovery of SHA-256-hashed passwords is bottlenecked by throughput and energy; existing hybrid CPU–FPGA accelerators reconfigure a large dynamic region on every message-length change. Approach: Redesigned the accelerator to isolate length-independent logic into a static region and confine padding/scheduling logic to a small dynamically-reconfigurable module; implemented block-transfer, control, padding/scheduling, and accelerating units in VHDL, unit-tested in Vivado. Key idea: Shrinking the reconfigured region is expected to cut PR overhead substantially while retaining inter-block parallelism via multiple accelerating units — design and unit-simulation only; no measured speedups are claimed.

Skills: VHDL, Vivado, FPGA, Partial Reconfiguration, SHA-256, Hardware Accelerator Design. Supervised by Dr. Somitra Sanadhya.
